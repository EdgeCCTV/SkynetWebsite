Protect Open Inventory Lots Along Every Metro Corridor

Dealership rows along Cobb Parkway, Thornton Road, Peachtree Industrial, and I-85 carry millions in outdoor inventory with open frontage that is impossible for a single walking guard to cover simultaneously. Thieves target key lockboxes, late-model trucks, wheel sets, and catalytic converters — often finishing in under two minutes when only passive cameras are recording.

Skynet Monitoring protects Metro Atlanta auto dealerships from our 24/7 Marietta Security Operations Center. As soon as analytics flag a person entering inventory rows or kneeling near a chassis after closing hours, a live operator reviews the feed, challenges the suspect over outdoor horn speakers by row and clothing description, and coordinates police dispatch if anyone stays on the lot.

Targeted Coverage for Dealership Risk Zones

  • Frontline & Overflow Inventory Lots: Active line-crossing detection on perimeter curbs, fence gaps, and overflow inventory rows.
  • Service Bays, Parts Drops & Body Shops: Watch overnight customer drop-off keys, parts deliveries, and tire storage bays.
  • Low-Angle Undercarriage Views: Catch crews sliding under trucks and vans before saws come out — not the next morning.
  • Dealer Morning Briefings: Receive clear incident clips and after-hours summaries so general managers know exactly what happened overnight.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you monitor our existing dealership camera system?

Often yes. We test existing row and service-drive cameras during your free night-coverage assessment and tell you honestly if any corner needs higher low-light clarity or speaker add-ons.

How do operators handle late service technicians or night porters?

Your lot manager approves scheduled service hours and an exception call-in procedure so authorized night porters or haulers are cleared without false police calls.

Why add live horn speakers on top of perimeter cameras?

Calling out an intruder by vehicle row and jacket color drives most crews off the lot within seconds — preventing broken glass, wheel damage, or cut exhaust systems before police arrive.
